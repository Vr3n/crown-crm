Core concept 2 of 3 · The sales entry

A sale is revenue earned.

What to record, when to record it, and why one sale makes more than one entry.

Press → or Space. Each press shows one new step. ← goes back.

1 · When is revenue earned?

Record revenue when you hand over the goods. Not when cash arrives.

OrderCustomer asks.
No entry yet.
→
Delivery + invoiceRevenue is recorded here.
→
PaymentCash or bank is recorded here.

An order is only a promise. It makes no entry. The invoice makes the sales entry.

In a cash sale, steps 2 and 3 happen on the same day. In a credit sale, payment comes later. The entry for the sale does not wait.

2 · Cash sale

Cash comes in. Revenue goes up.

A customer pays ₹10,000 in cash for goods.

Cash is a real account. It comes in. Debit it. Assets go up.

Sales is a nominal income account. Credit it. Income goes up, so equity goes up. The equation stays equal.

3 · Credit sale

No cash yet. The customer now owes you.

AccountDebitCredit

To Sales A/c5,000
Cash A/c Dr5,000
To Amit A/c5,000

Amit's account is a debtor. It is an asset: money owed to you. Revenue is already recorded.

When Amit pays, the asset changes form. Receivable goes down. Cash goes up. No new revenue.

4 · Cost of goods sold

A sale also removes stock from your shelf

You sell an SSD for ₹3,855.93. It cost you ₹3,200.

Sales
Sales ₹3,855.93
Split
Cost ₹3,200₹655.93
Entry B · costDebitCredit
Cost of Goods Sold A/c Dr3,200.00
To Inventory A/c3,200.00

Inventory is an asset. When it leaves, it goes down. Its cost becomes an expense.

Gross profit = sales − cost of goods sold = ₹655.93

5 · GST on a sale

GST is not your revenue. You hold it for the government.

Your sample bill: sale price ₹3,855.93 plus 18% GST. The customer pays ₹4,550.

Sales 3,855.93
AccountDebitCredit
Customer A/c Dr4,550.00
To Sales A/c3,855.93
To Output CGST A/c (9%)347.03
To Output SGST A/c (9%)347.03
To Round-off A/c0.01

Output GST is a liability. You collected it. You must pay it to the government.

3,855.93 + 347.03 + 347.03 = 4,549.99. The bill rounds to 4,550.00. The 0.01 goes to a round-off account.

6 · Output GST and input GST

GST you collect is a liability. GST you pay is an asset.

Same state

Split into Output CGST and Output SGST. Half each.

Other state

One account: Output IGST. The total tax is the same.

Input GST

GST on your purchases. It is an asset. It reduces what you pay.

Example monthAmount
Output GST collected on sales₹694.06
Input GST paid on purchases₹500.00
Net GST to pay₹194.06

Net GST payable = output GST − input GST. The input figure is an example.

7 · Sales return

A return reverses the sale. It does not edit it.

Credit note · customer returns the SSDDebitCredit
Sales Returns A/c Dr3,855.93
Output CGST A/c Dr347.03
Output SGST A/c Dr347.03
Round-off A/c Dr0.01
To Customer A/c4,550.00
Inventory A/c Dr3,200.00
To Cost of Goods Sold A/c3,200.00

Every line of the sale is mirrored. Debit and credit swap sides. The old bill stays as it was.

The goods return to stock. We reverse the cost entry too. Your 7-day return rule uses this.

8 · In your software

One sale voucher posts two entries. Staff enter one bill.

Entry A · revenue

Dr Customer (or Cash/Bank) = invoice total

Cr Sales = taxable value

Cr Output CGST + SGST, or IGST

Cr/Dr Round-off

Entry B · cost

Dr Cost of Goods Sold

Cr Inventory

Amount = quantity × cost from the stock movement.

Later · payment

Dr Cash/Bank, Cr Customer.

A bill allocation links it to the invoice.

Rules: both entries post in one transaction. Debits equal credits. A service item posts Entry A only.

Summary

Five sentences to remember

1

Record revenue at delivery and invoice, not at payment.

2

Cash sale: Dr Cash, Cr Sales. Credit sale: Dr Customer, Cr Sales.

3

A sale of goods also debits Cost of Goods Sold and credits Inventory.

4

Output GST is a liability, not revenue.

5

A return is a new reversing entry. Never edit the old bill.
