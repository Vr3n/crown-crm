02 The principle

Every report is arithmetic over rows written once, when the voucher was posted.

01 / WRITE

One write path

No screen writes a balance. A screen posts a document. The engine turns that document into ledger lines and stock movements.

If a second path existed, the two would drift.

02 / FREEZE

Facts are frozen

Every bill line copies the name, HSN, rate, discount and tax rate it used. Changing a master tomorrow cannot rewrite yesterday.

The item is a price list. The line is evidence.

03 / READ

Balances are queries

Outstanding, stock on hand, account balance and profit are computed from rows every time they are asked for.

Nothing to recalculate, nothing to disagree.

This single decision removes most accounting bugs before they can happen.

03 Cast

Nine entities carry every transaction. Nothing else is allowed to hold money.

CompanyWhose books. Holds GSTIN, state code, financial year.
PartyWho we deal with. Customer, supplier, or both.
AccountWhere money lands. One of five types.
ItemWhat we trade. HSN, unit, GST rate, price.
VoucherThe document. Sale, purchase, receipt, payment, note, journal.
Item lineWhat was traded, at what price, with what tax.
Ledger lineOne debit or one credit. Never both.
Stock movementOne quantity in or out. Never a balance.
AllocationWhich money settled which bill.

Why nine and not thirty

The gym had 38 tables because every concept grew its own lifecycle. Accounting needs six document types but one lifecycle: number, post, cancel, print, audit.

What is deliberately missing

No balance table, no outstanding table, no stock-on-hand table, no report cache. Each would be a copy of the truth, and copies drift.

Why two accounts, one party

A party without a ledger account cannot receive money. The account is created in the same transaction as the party.

Three consequences

HSN flows into the invoice line and the GST summary without retyping.

Unit allows four decimals of quantity, so kilograms and metres work as well as pieces.

Cost is what the sale will consume. Gross profit is not a guess.

Stock is never a number on the item. It is the sum of movements, which is why a correction can never silently overwrite history.

09 Step 5 · The arithmetic

Tax is computed per line, in integers, then grouped by rate.

01 · GROSS
Quantity times entered rate
2 × 450.00 = 900.00
02 · DISCOUNT
Basis points, half up
900.00 × 15.254% = 137.29
03 · TAXABLE
Net of discount
900.00 − 137.29 = 762.71
04 · SPLIT
Intra state or inter state
9% CGST + 9% SGST, or 18% IGST
05 · TAX
Per line, then per rate group
762.71 × 9% = 68.64 each side
06 · INVOICE
Round to the rupee, difference to round off
15,544.53 → 15,545.00, round off 0.47

Nine lines, one result

The printed bill differs by one paisa

Its 3215 group shows 136.90 per side. Nine percent of 1,521.20 is 136.908, which we round to 136.91. The total is identical because round off takes the difference.

Money is paise, quantity is scaled by 10,000, rates are basis points. No floating point anywhere in the path.

The inventory proof

Opening 0.00
+ Purchases 13,173.33
− Cost of goods sold 0.00
= Inventory 13,173.33

The same number appears in the stock summary and in the inventory account. If they differ, the integrity report says so.

What failure looks like

Missing credit: debits 13,173.33 against credits 0. Refused before a write.

Missing stock rows: the bill balances, the shelf is wrong. Caught by the stock summary, not by the trial balance.

The trial balance proves the money. The stock summary proves the shelf. Neither proves the other.

18 Contracts

Each table promises one thing and refuses the mistakes that break books.

TableGuaranteesRefuses
vouchersOne number per type, series and financial year. A type. A status.Editing a posted row. A duplicate number. A post without a type.
ledger_linesExactly one side filled. Debits equal credits per voucher.Both sides, neither side, a parent account, another company's account.
voucher_item_linesA frozen snapshot of name, HSN, rate, discount and tax.Reading today's item price into yesterday's bill.
stock_movementsOne direction per row. A unit cost on every issue.A quantity column, an overwrite, a movement without a voucher.
bill_allocationsPayment to bill links with a type and an amount.Over allocation, self allocation, linking two receipts.
partiesOne row per real business, flagged customer and or supplier.A duplicate GSTIN. A hard delete once a voucher exists.
Row level rules live in constraints: one side, positive amount, foreign keys. They are cheap and they never sleep.
Cross row rules live in the posting transaction: balance, allocation limits, numbering. They are tested with golden fixtures.
Constraints catch a bad row. The transaction catches a bad document. Tests catch the rules.

19 Failure modes

Skip a rule and the owner sees it in the shop, not in the code.

Rule skippedWhat happensWhat the owner experiences
Balance assertionA paisa lands on one side onlyThe trial balance is off. The accountant stops trusting every report.
Line snapshotA GST rate change rewrites old billsFiled returns no longer match the invoices the customer holds.
Allocation guardOne receipt pays two bills in fullThe shop chases a customer who already paid.
ImmutabilityTwo versions of the same invoice existNobody can answer which one was sent, or when it changed.
IdempotencyA double click posts twiceThe supplier is paid twice and the payable goes negative.
Stock movementInventory value drifts from the shelfMonth end becomes a spreadsheet and a guess.
