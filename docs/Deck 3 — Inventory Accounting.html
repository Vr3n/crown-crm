Core concept 3 of 3 · Inventory accounting

Stock is money on a shelf.

How to count it, value it, and move it into the profit statement.

Press → or Space. Each press shows one new step. ← goes back.

1 · Inventory is an asset

Unsold stock is an asset. A sale turns it into an expense.

While the SSD sits on the shelf, its cost is on the balance sheet.

When it sells, the cost leaves the balance sheet. It enters the income statement as COGS.

2 · COGS and gross profit

Cost of goods sold = what you had − what is left

Had
Beginning 50,000Purchases 120,000
Split
COGS 130,000Ending 40,000
Net sales 200,000−COGS 130,000=Gross profit ₹70,000

COGS = beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory.

Ending inventory stays as an asset. COGS is the part that was sold.

3 · Two ways to track

Periodic counts at the end. Perpetual updates every time.

Periodic

Purchase: Dr Purchases, Cr Supplier.

Sale: Dr Customer, Cr Sales. No cost entry.

At period end: count the stock. Compute COGS by the formula.

Perpetual

Purchase: Dr Inventory, Cr Supplier.

Sale: Dr Customer, Cr Sales. And Dr COGS, Cr Inventory.

The inventory balance is always known.

Your software is perpetual. Each stock movement posts an entry at once. You still count the shelf sometimes, to find errors.

4 · Cost flow

Same goods. Different prices. Which cost did you sell?

You buy 10 units at ₹100, then 10 units at ₹120. You sell 15 units at ₹150 each. Sales = ₹2,250.

FIFO

Oldest cost sells first.

COGS: 10×100 + 5×120 = ₹1,600

Ending: 5×120 = ₹600

Gross profit: ₹650

Weighted average

Average cost = 2,200 ÷ 20 = ₹110.

COGS: 15×110 = ₹1,650

Ending: 5×110 = ₹550

Gross profit: ₹600

LIFO

Newest cost sells first.

COGS: 10×120 + 5×100 = ₹1,700

Ending: 5×100 = ₹500

Gross profit: ₹550

COGS + ending inventory = ₹2,200 in every method. The method only splits the same cost differently.

When prices rise, FIFO shows the highest profit. LIFO is not allowed under India's AS 2 and Ind AS 2. Offer FIFO and weighted average. Confirm against current standards.

6 · Valuation rule

Report stock at the lower of cost and net realisable value

An item cost ₹100. It now sells for ₹90. Selling costs ₹10.

Cost
Cost ₹100
Net value
NRV ₹80 = 90 − 10Loss ₹20
Write-down entryDebitCredit
Inventory Write-down A/c (expense) Dr20
To Inventory A/c20

NRV = expected selling price − cost to sell.

Stock is lower than cost. Book the loss now, not when you sell. Check item by item. Never value above cost.

7 · Physical count

A count finds differences. An entry fixes the books.

The books say 100 units at ₹110 = ₹11,000. You count 97.

97 counted
Shortage: 3 × ₹110DebitCredit
Stock Shortage A/c (loss) Dr330
To Inventory A/c330

3 units are missing. Cause: theft, damage, or a wrong entry.

An excess works the other way: Dr Inventory, Cr Stock Gain. Never overwrite the quantity. Post an adjustment.

8 · In your software

The stock records and the ledger must always agree

Movements

Append-only stock_movements. Each has quantity and unit cost.

Costing method

Choose FIFO or weighted average per company. Save the cost on each issue.

Auto posting

Sale: COGS = sum of issued cost. Post Dr COGS, Cr Inventory.

Control check

Inventory ledger balance = sum of (quantity × cost). Test it daily.

Add three voucher types: stock journal (transfers), physical count (adjustments), and write-down (NRV).
