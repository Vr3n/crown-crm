Crown Books · 1.0

Crown Books keeps the accounts of one business on this computer. It answers three questions: who owes us, who do we owe, and do the books balance. This guide shows the actual screens and the buttons to press.

Before you start

  • Business name, address, state and GSTIN.
  • Owner name, email and a password of at least 8 characters.
  • The date your books should begin.
  • Customer and supplier list, with phone and GSTIN where known.
  • Item list with HSN or SAC, unit, GST rate, sale and purchase price.
  • Opening stock, party balances and cash or bank balances.

How to read a step

Button and field names appear in bold exactly as they appear on screen. Numbers and voucher numbers appear in monospace.

A red number on a screenshot matches a numbered step or key beside it. Reference figures use the key printed under the picture.

Tip Do one chapter at a time. The first five steps in chapter 1 are enough to post your first invoice. Everything else can wait until you need it.
The one rule that prevents most mistakes Nothing is saved until you press Post. Closing the screen or pressing Cancel discards the entry. A posted voucher is never edited: cancel it with a reason and enter a new one.

Chapter 01Crown Books · 1.0
01

Start here

Open the app, create the company, set the voucher numbers, and walk the five steps from empty books to a balanced trial balance. By the end of this chapter you will know where everything lives.

In this chapterOpen · First run · Numbering · The tour · Sign in · The screen · Voucher anatomy · First five steps

1.1Open the app

Crown Books is installed on this computer. Open it from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut. The app checks that this computer is the one the books were installed for. If you see “This computer is not authorised”, stop and contact your administrator; the application is not designed to be copied to another machine.

There is no internet connection, no cloud account and no website sign-in. Everything is stored in a local database file on this computer.

1.2First-run setup

The first time the app opens, it asks for the company and the owner account. This happens once. You can change the company details later in Admin → Company.

  1. Business name, owner name and phone. The name is printed on invoices; the owner is the first user and can do everything.
  2. Owner email. This becomes your sign-in name. Use an address you check.
  3. Password. At least 8 characters, stored hashed on this machine. There is no “forgot password” email; keep it somewhere safe.
  4. State and GSTIN. The state decides CGST + SGST or IGST. Enter the GSTIN exactly as registered, without spaces.
  5. Books begin. The first day Crown Books records transactions. Read 2.2 before choosing a mid-year date.
  6. Click Create company and sign in.
Books begin is not the financial year The financial year is the reporting period, usually 1 April to 31 March. Books begin is the day this app starts keeping the ledger. Starting fresh on 1 April? Both dates can be 1 April. Migrating in October? Books begin is the day after your last entry in the old system; the financial year is still the current April-to-March year.

1.3Number your vouchers

The company is created and you are signed in. Before the dashboard, step 1 of 2 asks you to confirm the numbering. Every document type has its own row: sales invoice, purchase bill, receipt, payment, credit note, debit note and journal.

Column What it does
Series The short code stored on the voucher, for example INV. Changing it starts a new series; older vouchers keep theirs.
Prefix / Suffix Text around the number, for example INV/. Both may contain date tokens.
Width How many digits the number uses, from 1 to 8. A larger value is never cut.
Start The first number in a new series or financial year.
Reset Yearly, monthly or never. Yearly restarts on 1 April.
Manual Allows typing the number when posting. It must still be higher than the last one used.
Next A live preview from the same formatter the posting engine uses, with the GST limit of 16 characters.

The date tokens are {YYYY}, {YY}, {MM}, {DD}, {YYYYMM}, {FY} and {FY_SHORT}. A prefix of INV/{YYYYMM} with width 3 produces INV/202607001.

Numbers are assigned when a voucher is posted, not when the entry form is opened. Changing a prefix or width affects the next number only; a posted voucher keeps the number it was given. Click Save numbering to continue, or open Admin → Voucher numbering later.

1.4The guided tour

Step 2 of 2 is a checklist, not a simulation. It lists the five steps from empty books to a balanced trial balance, and each Open button goes to the real screen.

The counter and progress bar read the same rows as the dashboard. A step is ticked only after you add a party, add an item, post a purchase or post a sale. The last step, reading the trial balance, is a pointer rather than a stored tick.

Click Skip the tour to go straight to the dashboard, or Finish and open dashboard when you are done. The Set up the books card on the dashboard keeps the same checklist, with the next step and a Continue link.

1.5Sign in again

After setup, every launch shows the sign-in screen. Enter the owner email and password and click Sign in. To leave, open the account menu at the top right and choose Sign out. Closing the window is also safe: every posted voucher is already in the database.

1.6The screen

Every page has the same frame: a dark navigation rail on the left, a thin top bar, and the page itself. The rail is grouped by the work you are doing, not by database tables.

Rail group What it is for
Dashboard Receivables, payables, cash and bank, vouchers posted, and the setup checklist.
Books Day Book, Sales, Purchases, Receipts, Payments and Journals: the documents.
Parties All Parties, Customers and Suppliers. One record per business.
Items Items and Stock Summary: goods, services and what is on hand.
Reports Receivables, Payables, Ageing, Trial Balance, Profit and Loss, Balance Sheet. The sales and purchase registers open from their list pages.
Masters Chart of Accounts, Tax Rates and Units.

The top bar shows the page name and the current financial year. The search box in the middle finds parties, items, vouchers, accounts and screens from anywhere: click it or press Ctrl+K. The theme button switches between the paper and dark palettes, and the account menu at the right holds the signed-in user and Sign out.

Rail items are gated by the signed-in user’s role. A Billing Clerk, for example, does not see Admin at all, and sees only Tax Rates and Units under Masters. Typing a protected address such as Company or Users sends them back to the dashboard. The owner account created at first run can see and do everything. Chapter 10 lists the seeded roles and what each one can reach.

1.7The anatomy of a voucher

A voucher is any document that changes the books: an invoice, a bill, a receipt, a payment, a journal or a contra. Every entry screen uses the same four-part pattern. Once you know one, you know all of them.

01HeaderParty, date, due date, reference and place of supply.
→
02LinesItem, description, quantity, rate, discount and GST.
→
03TotalsTaxable value, tax, round off and the final total.
→
04PostThe server checks, numbers and writes the voucher.

The party field on a sale or purchase is a search box, not a plain list. Click it or press F4 and type part of the name, phone number or GSTIN. The same applies to the item on each line: choosing an item fills its description, HSN, unit, rate and GST rate. The footer of the entry screen lists the other keys: Alt+A adds a line, Ctrl+Enter posts, and Esc goes back.

1.8Your first five steps

For a complete cycle, do these five things in order. The rest of the guide explains each one in detail.

  1. Create the company in first-run setup. Use 1 April as books begin if you are starting a fresh year. Confirm the voucher numbers and skip or follow the tour.
  2. Add a customer and a supplier under Parties. One record each is enough to begin.
  3. Add one item under Items. Enter its HSN, GST rate and sale price.
  4. Post a purchase bill under Purchases. This brings stock in and creates the supplier payable.
  5. Post a sales invoice under Sales. Then open it and click Print.

Chapter 02Crown Books · 1.0
02

Company and opening balances

The company profile is printed on every document. The chart of accounts is the list of ledgers behind the reports. Opening balances are the one-time bridge from your old books.

In this chapterCompany settings · Books begin · Chart of accounts · Opening balances

2.1Company settings

Open Admin → Company. The page has four sections: identity, financial year, stock and rounding, and invoice terms. The first two are printed on documents; the last two control costing and rounding.

Registered name
The name printed on invoices, usually the legal or trading name.
GSTIN, PAN, state code
Registration details. The state code is two digits, for example 27 for Maharashtra.
Address, city, pincode
The address printed above the invoice lines.
Financial year starts
The month your reporting year starts. April is the default for India.
Books begin date
The first day this app records transactions. See 2.2.
Costing method
Keep Weighted average. This edition values stock at weighted average cost.
Round off
How the invoice total is rounded. Invoice total is the usual choice.
Default terms
Saved with the company, but not printed by the current invoice layout.
Leave this unticked Allow selling below stock on hand is recorded as a setting but the current build does not enforce a stock guard. Keep it unticked and check the stock summary before a large sale.

2.2What “books begin” means

Books begin is the first day Crown Books is responsible for the ledger. Before that date, the history lives in your old system, your accountant’s file, or nowhere. At that date you bring in one opening position: what you own, what you owe, and what customers owe you.

It is not the financial year. A business can have a financial year from 1 April to 31 March and still begin its Crown Books ledger on 1 October after migrating from Tally. An October invoice still belongs to the current April-to-March year.

Situation What to enter
Starting a new business The day you want the books to start, often 1 April or the day of the first sale.
Fresh financial year 1 April of that year. Opening balances are the previous 31 March closing balances.
Migrating mid-year The day after the last entry in the old system. Opening balances are as at the close of the day before.
Only using Crown Books for sales Still use the real start date. Do not enter a later date just to avoid old data.
Opening balances are the day before If the old system is closed on 30 September and books begin is 1 October, the opening balances are the position at the close of 30 September. Post the opening voucher dated 1 October, then start entering October transactions.

2.3Chart of accounts

Every voucher writes to ledgers. The chart of accounts is the list of those ledgers, grouped into assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses. Crown Books seeds a working set so you do not have to create one from scratch.

The four cards split the ledger into Assets, Liabilities, Income and Expenses. The Balance by group chart ranks the groups by value, so a large balance in an unexpected group is visible before you read the rows. It reads the same accounts as the table below.

  1. Click New account.
  2. Enter a Name, for example Office Expenses.
  3. Choose a Group. It decides the nature, for example Indirect Expenses.
  4. Check the Type. It follows the group but can be adjusted.
  5. Leave the opening balance at zero unless you are setting up the opening position.
  6. Click Add account.

Reserved ledgers have a fixed job: sales, purchases, GST, inventory, cost of goods sold, round off and the opening difference. Add your own ledgers for rent, salaries or travel without touching them.

The chart is gated by the company.view permission. A user without it does not see Masters in the rail, and the page cannot be opened by typing its address.

2.4Opening balances

Opening balances come from three places: the party record, the account record and the item record. They are collected into one opening voucher so that the trial balance stays balanced.

  1. Add party opening balances in the party records. Positive means the party owes you; negative means you owe the party.
  2. Add the opening balance and side on each bank, cash, loan or capital account.
  3. Add opening quantity and rate on each goods item. Services do not hold stock.
  4. Open Admin → Company, scroll to Opening balances, and choose the Opening date.
  5. Click Post opening balances. One OPN/0001 voucher is created.
  6. Open the trial balance and check that debits equal credits.

2.5Opening balance checklist

  • The old system is closed and reconciled on the day before books begin.
  • Every customer and supplier balance has the correct Dr or Cr side.
  • Cash, bank, loans and capital are entered on the account records.
  • Goods have opening quantity and rate, or zero if you are bringing purchase history.
  • The opening date matches the day before the first new transaction.
  • The trial balance says “Debits equal credits” and Opening Balance Difference is zero or explained.

After the opening voucher is posted, the opening fields are locked. Corrections go through a journal, not by editing the voucher.

Chapter 03Crown Books · 1.0
03

Customers and suppliers

One record per business. The same business can be a customer and a supplier. The balance column tells you whether they owe you or you owe them.

In this chapterOne record per business · Create a party · Edit and deactivate · Read the list · Credit terms

3.1One record per business

A party is a customer, a supplier, or both. Use one record for a business even if you buy from and sell to them. The role decides which ledger accounts the party uses; you can change it later by editing the party.

The balance is shown as Dr or Cr. For a customer, Dr means they owe you. For a supplier, Cr means you owe them. A negative customer balance or a positive supplier balance is possible after an advance or an overpayment; it is not automatically an error.

3.2Create a party

Open Parties → All Parties and click New party.

1 2

1 Click Parties in the sidebar, then All Parties. 2 Click New party.

  1. Name. The trading name you will recognise in lists, for example Gupta Traders.
  2. Phone and Email. Optional, but useful when chasing a bill.
  3. GSTIN and State code. 15 characters and two digits. The state decides CGST + SGST or IGST on a sale.
  4. Credit days and Credit limit. 30 means the invoice is due 30 days after the invoice date.
  5. Opening balance. Positive if the party owes you, negative if you owe the party. It can be corrected until the opening voucher is posted.
  6. Role. Customer, Supplier, or both. You can change it later by editing the party.
  7. Click Add party.

3.3Edit and deactivate

Use Edit on a row to correct a phone number, address, credit days, credit limit, role or opening balance. The opening balance stops being editable once the opening voucher is posted; use a journal after that. If a party is no longer used, click Deactivate: the history stays in the books, and the party stops appearing in new-entry lists.

Tip Deactivate a party instead of renaming it to “OLD”. Reports and old vouchers keep the original name and history, and the list stays readable.

3.4Read the party list

The top of the page answers three questions before you scroll: how much is owed to you, how much is overdue, and which customers are over their credit limit. The Open bills by age chart splits receivables and payables into Not due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ days. Top receivables ranks the largest outstanding balances, so a single customer holding most of the money is obvious.

Search matches name, phone and GSTIN. The role buttons filter to All, Customers or Suppliers. Show inactive reveals deactivated records. The Credit days column is the default payment term, not the age of the current balance.

3.5Credit days and credit limit

Credit days are copied to the due date when you choose the party on an invoice. You can change the due date for an individual invoice. Ageing reports use the due date by default, so a wrong credit term makes a bill look overdue earlier or later than it really is.

Credit limit is a reference figure for your team. In this edition it does not stop an invoice from being posted. If a customer is near the limit, the person entering the sale must check.

Role Use it when
Customer You sell to them. They appear in Sales and Receivables.
Supplier You buy from them. They appear in Purchases and Payables.
Both You buy and sell to the same business. One record keeps the net balance in one place.
Chapter 04Crown Books · 1.0
04

Items and stock

An item is anything you buy or sell: a physical product with stock, or a service with no stock. The item carries the HSN or SAC, the unit, the GST rate and the default prices.

In this chapterGoods and services · Create an item · Opening stock · Item list · Stock summary · How stock moves

4.1Goods and services

A goods item carries stock. Buying it increases quantity, selling it decreases quantity, and the stock summary shows what is left. A service item has no stock; it is used for maintenance, consulting or any other work billed by quantity, hours or a flat rate.

4.2Create an item

Open Items → Items and click New item.

1 2

1 Click Items in the sidebar, then Items. 2 Click New item.

  1. Name and SKU. The name appears on the invoice; the SKU is your internal code.
  2. HSN / SAC. The GST classification. Goods use HSN, services use SAC.
  3. Unit and GST rate. 0, 5, 12, 18 or 28 percent. The rate is copied to a new invoice line but can be changed there.
  4. Service item (no stock). Tick for services.
  5. Sale price and Purchase price. Defaults, not locked prices.
  6. Opening qty and Opening rate. Only for goods you already hold. Editable until the item has stock movements.
  7. Reorder level. A reference for purchasing; it does not create an alert in this edition.
  8. Click Add item.

4.3Opening stock

Opening quantity and rate are included in the one-time opening voucher. They are not a purchase bill. If you enter opening stock and then also enter a purchase bill for the same goods, stock and cost will be counted twice.

Choose one method Opening stock only: enter what you hold now and start buying from today. Full purchase history: leave opening stock at zero and enter the old purchase bills. Do not mix the two for the same goods.

4.4The item list

The four cards across the top are inventory value at cost, the number of items, how many are at or below their reorder level, and how many are out of stock. Stock value by item ranks the largest inventory balances, so the item holding most of your money is visible before you read the table. The Quick filters jump straight to low stock, out of stock or services.

The list shows HSN or SAC, unit, GST rate, stock on hand, sale price and purchase price. Use the search box for name, SKU or HSN. Edit changes the descriptive fields and default prices; opening quantity and rate stay fixed. Deactivate hides an item from new entries without deleting history.

4.5Stock summary

Open Items → Stock Summary. This is a computed report, not a data-entry screen. It reads every posted purchase, sale and return.

Quantity is what is left. Value is quantity multiplied by the weighted average cost of the item. The total at the bottom is the inventory value that appears on the balance sheet. Services are not shown because they hold no stock.

The Stock value by item chart ranks the largest balances, and Value concentration shows how much of the total each top item represents. Both read the same rows as the table, so they move together after every posted purchase or sale.

4.6How stock moves

Action Effect on stock Effect on the books
Post a purchase bill Quantity increases. Creditor increases, inventory increases, input GST is recorded.
Post a sales invoice Quantity decreases. Customer owes you, sales and output GST increase, cost of goods sold is recorded.
Cancel a purchase or sale The original movement is reversed. The ledger effect is reversed; the voucher stays with status Cancelled.
Enter opening stock Quantity starts at the opening figure. Inventory and the opening difference are recorded once.
Why the stock value can change without a new purchase The costing method is weighted average. When a new purchase arrives at a different rate, the average cost of the remaining stock changes, so the value of the same quantity can move.

Buying: bills and payments

A purchase bill brings stock in and creates what you owe the supplier. A payment reduces that balance. The payables report tells you what is still open and when it is due.

In this chapterThe buying flow · New purchase bill · What posting does · Pay a supplier · Payables · Purchase register

5.1The buying flow

01Purchase billStock comes in. The supplier becomes a creditor.
→
02PaymentMoney goes out. Allocate it to the bill it settles.
→
03PayablesWhat is still owed, split into not due and overdue.

5.2New purchase bill

Open Books → Purchases and click New bill. The screen is a bill, not a payment. Enter what the supplier billed you.

1 2

1 Click Purchases in the sidebar. 2 Click New bill.

  1. Party. Click the supplier box, or press F4, and type part of the name, phone or GSTIN. The selected supplier shows the current balance and credit days.
  2. Date and Due date. The bill date decides the financial year and the register period. The due date fills from credit days.
  3. Reference. The supplier’s bill number, for example TC/26-27/604.
  4. Place of supply. The supplier’s state. Same state gives CGST + SGST; a different state gives IGST.
  5. Add line for each item. Choose the item or type a free-text description.
  6. Enter Qty, Rate, Disc % and GST %. HSN and unit fill from the item.
  7. Check Taxable value, the tax split, Round off and Total.
  8. Add a Narration that will make sense in six months.
  9. Click Post bill. The number, for example PUR/0001, is assigned at that moment.

5.3What posting does

  • The supplier’s payable increases by the bill total.
  • Inventory increases by the taxable value of goods lines; services post to the expense or purchase ledger without stock.
  • Input CGST, SGST or IGST is recorded according to the place of supply.
  • The voucher gets a permanent number and appears in the purchase register and payables.

To print the posted bill, open it from Purchases and click Print. The print dialog also offers Save as PDF.

The bill is not the payment Posting a purchase bill does not move money. It records the obligation. Use a payment voucher when the money actually leaves.

5.4Pay a supplier

Open Books → Payments and click New payment. A payment can settle one bill, several bills, or stay on account as an advance.

1 2

1 Click Payments in the sidebar. 2 Click New payment.

  1. Party. Choose the supplier from the list. Each row shows the current balance, and the line under the list repeats it.
  2. Date. The date money left, not the bill date.
  3. Amount. The total paid, for example 25,000.
  4. Method and Reference. NEFT, UPI, cheque or cash, and the bank reference.
  5. Open bills lists unpaid bills with due dates and outstanding amounts.
  6. Enter the amount against the bill in Allocate. Leave the rest unallocated if it is an advance.
  7. Check Paid, Allocated and On account at the bottom.
  8. Click Post payment.
Situation What to do
Pay one bill in full Allocate the full outstanding amount to that bill.
Part payment Allocate less than the outstanding. The rest stays open.
Pay several bills Enter an allocation against each bill in the same payment.
Advance Leave the allocation blank. The money sits on account until a bill arrives.
Pay more than the bill Allocate the bill amount and leave the excess on account.
Cash and bank in this edition Receipts and payments post to the Cash ledger. The Method list is a label on the entry screen. To move money between cash and bank, use Journals → New contra after the receipt or payment. Bank reconciliation is not part of this edition.

5.5Payables

Open Reports → Payables. The report shows who you owe, split into amounts not yet due and amounts past the due date.

As of is the date you are looking at. Basis decides whether a bill is measured by its bill date or its due date. Use Due date for collection and payment planning; use Bill date when you want the age of the document.

5.6Purchase register

Open Reports → Purchase Register. It lists every purchase bill in the period with taxable value, GST, round off and total.

Selling: invoices and receipts

An invoice records what a customer owes. A receipt records what they paid. Receivables and ageing tell you who to chase and how old the bill is.

In this chapterThe selling flow · New invoice · GST and place of supply · Print · Receipt · Receivables and ageing · Sales register

6.1The selling flow

01Sales invoiceCustomer owes you. Stock goes out. GST is recorded.
→
02ReceiptMoney comes in. Allocate it to the invoice it settles.
→
03ReceivablesWhat is still owed, with ageing and due dates.

6.2New sales invoice

Open Books → Sales and click New invoice. Every sale is against a customer; a cash sale is recorded as an invoice followed by a receipt.

1 2

1 Click Sales in the sidebar. 2 Click New invoice.

  1. Party. Click the customer box, or press F4, and type part of the name, phone or GSTIN. The selected customer shows their balance, credit days and GSTIN.
  2. Date and Due date. The invoice date decides the financial year and the sales register.
  3. Place of supply. The customer’s state. Same state as your company gives CGST + SGST; a different state gives IGST.
  4. Add line for each item. Pick a saved item to fill its description, HSN, unit, rate and GST rate, or type a free-text description.
  5. Enter Qty, Rate, Disc % and GST %.
  6. Check the totals and add a Narration.
  7. Click Post invoice. The number, for example INV/0001, is assigned on posting.
Place of supply is the most common GST mistake The place of supply defaults to your company’s state. For an out-of-state customer, change it to the customer’s state before posting. If you leave it on your own state, the invoice will charge CGST + SGST instead of IGST and the GST return will be wrong.

6.3GST and place of supply

GST is charged per line. The place of supply decides how the same 18 percent is split.

Place of supply What is charged Example
Same state as the company CGST + SGST, half the rate each. Company in 27 (Maharashtra), customer in 27. 18% becomes 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
Different state IGST at the full rate. Company in 27, customer in 29 (Karnataka). 18% becomes 18% IGST.

The tax is calculated on the taxable value after the line discount. The total is rounded according to the company setting, usually at the invoice total. The summary on the right shows taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST, round off and total before you post.

6.4Print or save the invoice

Open Sales, click the invoice number, and use Print. In the system print dialog, choose a printer or Save as PDF. The printed page carries the company name and GSTIN, the customer, the invoice number and date, the line items with HSN, the tax split and the total.

6.5Receipt from a customer

Open Books → Receipts and click New receipt. A receipt can settle one invoice, several invoices, or stay on account as an advance.

1 2

1 Click Receipts in the sidebar. 2 Click New receipt.

  1. Party. Choose the customer from the list. Each row shows the current balance, and the line under the list repeats it.
  2. Date. The date money was received.
  3. Amount. The total received.
  4. Method and Reference. UPI, NEFT, cheque or cash, and the reference number.
  5. Open invoices lists unpaid invoices with due dates and outstanding amounts.
  6. Enter the amount against the invoice in Allocate.
  7. Check Received, Allocated and On account.
  8. Click Post receipt.

Advances and part payments. A receipt larger than the open invoice leaves the excess on account. A receipt with no allocation is an advance. When the next invoice is posted, use a new receipt, or adjust the advance through a journal if your accountant prefers.

6.6Receivables and ageing

Open Reports → Receivables for the customer totals, and Reports → Ageing for the age buckets.

Receivables shows not yet due and overdue per customer. Ageing splits the overdue amount into 30-day buckets. Use Basis to age by due date (default) or bill date; a bill not yet due stays in Not due even if the invoice is old.

Ageing by party

Who to chase first Start with the largest amount in 61–90 and 91–180, not the largest total. Then check 31–60 for customers who are about to become a bigger problem. A 180+ balance needs a decision: collect, write off, or ask the accountant.

6.7Sales register

The sales register lists every invoice in a period with taxable value, GST, round off and total. It is the first check before a GST return or a management review.

  • Every invoice in the period is present, and none is cancelled by mistake.
  • The total GST agrees with the output tax in the trial balance.

Adjustments and corrections

The books keep a history. A mistake is corrected by cancelling the voucher and entering a new one, or by posting a journal. Nothing is deleted.

In this chapterPosted means posted · Cancel a voucher · Journal · Contra · Journals list · Ask the accountant

7.1Posted means posted

A posted voucher is part of the audit trail. You cannot edit its lines, party, date or amount. If it is wrong, cancel it with a reason and enter a fresh voucher. The cancelled voucher stays visible with status Cancelled, its number is not reused, and its ledger and stock effect is reversed.

7.2Cancel a voucher

  1. Open the voucher from Sales, Purchases, Receipts, Payments, Day Book or a report link.
  2. Click Cancel voucher at the top right.
  3. Enter a Reason of at least three characters. “Wrong party” is enough; “test” is not useful.
  4. Click Cancel voucher in the dialog. The status becomes Cancelled.
  5. Enter the corrected voucher from the beginning.
Do not cancel just to hide a bill Cancellation reverses the ledger and stock effect, but the voucher remains in the day book with its reason. It is for corrections, not for removing a real transaction from history.

7.3Journal

A journal is a manual double entry. Use it for adjustments that are not a sale, purchase, receipt or payment: owner capital, depreciation, an expense paid from cash, or an accountant’s correction. Every journal needs at least two lines, and total debits must equal total credits. The screen shows Balanced only when they match; Post stays disabled until then.

1 2

1 Click Journals in the sidebar. 2 Click New journal.

  1. Date. The date of the adjustment.
  2. Account to debit, with the amount. For an expense, debit the expense.
  3. Account to credit, with the same amount. Usually Cash or Bank.
  4. Enter a Narration that names the reason.
  5. Check that the dialog shows Balanced.
  6. Click Post.
Before you journal Write down the two account names and the amount before you open the screen. If you cannot name both accounts, stop and ask the accountant. A journal entered to “see if it works” is harder to clean up than a phone call.
Example Debit Credit
Owner introduces capital Cash Owner Capital
Office expense paid in cash Office Expenses Cash
Depreciation for the month Depreciation Accumulated Depreciation
Correct an over-posted expense The account that was wrongly debited The expense account

7.4Contra

A contra moves money between two cash or bank accounts. The common case is depositing cash into the bank. It is not a receipt or a payment, so it does not affect a customer or supplier.

1 2

1 Click Journals in the sidebar. 2 Click New contra.

  1. Open Books → Journals and click New contra.
  2. Choose the From account, usually Cash.
  3. Choose the To account, usually Bank.
  4. Enter the Date and Amount.
  5. Add a Narration, for example “Cash deposited into the bank”.
  6. Click Post.

7.5The journals list

The Journals page lists both manual journals and contra transfers, newest first. The Type column tells you which one it is; the Number column is the voucher you can quote to your accountant.

7.6When to ask your accountant

  • Opening balances do not tie to the old trial balance.
  • A GST return is due and the output or input tax looks wrong.
  • You need depreciation, prepaid expenses, provisions or any year-end adjustment.
  • A party balance is negative or a supplier balance is positive and you cannot explain it.
  • The trial balance does not say “Debits equal credits.”
The mistake What to do
Wrong party, date, item or amount on a posted bill Cancel it with a reason and enter the correct voucher.
Wrong GST rate or place of supply on a posted invoice Cancel and re-enter. Do not patch it with a journal; the GST return needs the corrected invoice.
Expense posted to the wrong ledger Post a journal: debit the correct expense, credit the wrong one.
Owner put money into the business Journal: debit Cash or Bank, credit Owner Capital.
Cash deposited into the bank Contra: from Cash, to Bank. Not a receipt or payment.
Chapter 08Crown Books · 1.0
08

Reports

Reports are reads of posted vouchers. They do not store totals, so a cancelled voucher disappears from the report and a new one appears immediately.

In this chapterHow reports work · Day book · Trial balance · Profit and loss · Balance sheet · Report map

8.1How reports work

Every report reads the posted vouchers for the current company. A cancelled voucher is excluded from the ledger effect. The date controls mean different things:

Control Meaning
From / To A period, for example 1 April to 22 October. Used by profit and loss, balance sheet and registers.
As of A single date. Used by receivables, payables and ageing: what was open on that day.
Basis Age by due date (default) or by bill date. Due date is better for collection; bill date is better for document age.
Three checks before you trust a report Is the voucher posted, not cancelled? Is the date range or as-of date the one you meant? Does the trial balance still say “Debits equal credits”?

8.2Report map

Report Question it answers When to use it
Receivables Who owes us, and how much is overdue? Weekly collection review.
Ageing How old are the unpaid bills? Prioritise the oldest debts.
Payables Who do we owe, and what is due? Weekly payment run.
Day book What was posted on a date? Find a voucher or check an entry.
Sales register What did we sell, and what GST did we charge? Before a GST return or a sales review.
Purchase register What did we buy, and what input GST was recorded? Before a GST return or a cost review.
Trial balance Do the books balance? Month end, before any report is trusted.
Profit and loss Did we make money this period? Monthly management review.
Balance sheet What do we own and owe? Month end, bank or investor review.

8.3Day book

The day book lists every voucher in date order, whatever its type. It is the first place to look when you cannot find a document.

The date range defaults to the current month. Change From and To for an older period, use Search for a number or party, and filter by Status to see only posted or cancelled documents.

8.4Trial balance

The trial balance lists every account with a debit or credit balance. If the debit total equals the credit total, the ledger is internally consistent. It does not prove that the business made a profit; it proves that every voucher had two sides.

Read it by group. Assets and expenses normally sit on the debit side; liabilities, equity and income on the credit side. A party can appear on either side because the balance is net.

8.5Profit and loss

Profit and loss answers one question: did the business make money in this period? It shows income, expenses and net profit. The period is controlled by From and To.

Sales is shown net of returns. Cost of goods sold is the weighted average cost of the stock that left. Expenses include round off and any journals posted to expense accounts. A loss is shown in red.

8.6Balance sheet

The balance sheet is a photograph at a date: what the business owns, what it owes, and the owner’s stake. It must satisfy Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

Inventory is valued at weighted average cost. Customer and supplier balances are net. Current period profit is added to equity automatically, so the sheet balances without a manual closing entry.

Backup and safety

The books live in one SQLite file on this computer. A backup is a copy of that file. Restore is deliberately manual so that a live database is never swapped underneath the app.

In this chapterCreate a backup · Restore · When to back up · Safety rules · This computer only

9.1Create a backup

Open Admin → Backup and click Create backup. The app writes a timestamped copy, for example crown-books-2026-10-22T09-15-00-000Z.db, into the backup folder next to the database.

  1. Click Create backup.
  2. Confirm the new row in the list. It shows the file name, creation time and size.
  3. Copy that file to a different drive or a USB stick. A backup on the same laptop is not enough.

A backup is a complete copy, not a delta. Restore is done outside the app, with the app closed.

Close the app first Restoring while Crown Books is open can corrupt the database. Close the app completely, including any window in the taskbar, before replacing the file.

9.2Restore a backup

  1. Create a fresh backup of the current books, even if they are wrong. It is your safety net.
  2. Close Crown Books.
  3. Open the app data folder. On Windows this is usually %APPDATA%\crown-books.
  4. Rename the current crown-books.db to crown-books.before-restore.db.
  5. Copy the chosen backup from the backups folder into the app data folder.
  6. Rename the copy to crown-books.db.
  7. Open Crown Books and sign in. Check the dashboard, the trial balance and a recent invoice.

The backup folder is usually %APPDATA%\crown-books\backups. If your installation uses a different folder, ask your administrator before moving files.

9.3When to back up

When Why
Before the first real bill So you can return to an empty, known state.
Every day after posting The day’s work is the most expensive thing to lose.
Before a GST return Freeze the numbers you are about to file.
Before a restore or upgrade Keep a copy of the current state even if it is imperfect.
Every Friday, off the machine Protect against disk failure, theft or ransomware.
At month end A clean copy for the accountant.

9.4Safety rules

  • Do not edit the database with another program while Crown Books is open.
  • Do not copy the app folder to another computer. The install lock will refuse to run, and the data may not be the same.
  • Do not restore a backup over a live database. Close the app first.
  • Do not keep every backup on the same laptop. Use a second drive or a USB stick.

9.5This computer only

Crown Books is offline by design. There is no cloud sync, no remote login and no automatic off-site copy. That is a feature for privacy and a risk for disaster recovery. The backup routine is the disaster recovery plan.

The 3-2-1 rule, in plain language Keep 3 copies, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 copy off the computer. For a small shop, that can be the live database, a daily backup on the laptop, and a weekly copy on a USB drive kept elsewhere.

10.3People, roles and permissions

Every person signs in with their own email and password. A role is a named bundle of permissions; a person gets exactly one role. Open Admin → Users and Roles to add people, reset a password, disable an account, or change what a role may do.

Role What it can do
Owner · Admin Full access, including people, roles, voucher numbering, company settings and backups. Both are locked system roles; they cannot be edited or deleted. At least one active Owner must remain.
Accountant Parties, items, vouchers, receipts, payments, journals and reports, plus exports and backups. Can cancel a voucher and edit the chart of accounts. Can see people and roles but not change them.
Billing Clerk Raises sales invoices and purchase bills, records receipts, and views reports. Cannot cancel a voucher, edit accounts, open backups, or change company settings.
Auditor Read-only. Sees parties, items, vouchers, receipts, payments, journals and every report, and can export them. Cannot post, edit or delete anything.

These four are starting points, not fixed rules. A non-system role can be edited, and a new role can be created from the Roles tab. The role editor groups permissions by domain: company, people, parties, items, vouchers, receipts and payments, journals, reports, and backup. A person with user.manage can add people; a person with role.manage can change roles.

Permissions are enforced in the main process Hiding a button is not the protection. Every command is checked again on the server side, so a role without voucher.cancel cannot cancel a voucher even if the screen is reached some other way. Nobody can change their own role or disable their own account, and nobody can grant a permission they do not hold themselves.

Changes to people and roles are written to the audit trail. Disabling a person keeps their name on the vouchers they posted; it only stops them signing in.

10.4Messages and fixes

Message or symptom What it means and what to do
“Debits equal credits.” The banner at the bottom of the trial balance. Every posted voucher has two sides; no action.
“The books do not balance.” Stop. Take a backup and ask your accountant; do not post more entries to hide it.
“Opening balances have already been posted” Only one opening voucher is allowed. Use a journal for corrections.
“Choose customer, supplier, or both” The party has no role. Tick Customer, Supplier, or both in the party form; you can change it later by editing the party.
“GSTIN must be 15 characters” Count the characters. Remove spaces and dashes.
“State code: two digits” Use 27, not “Maharashtra” or “MH”.
“Password must be at least 8 characters” Use a longer password. There is no email reset in this edition.
“This computer is not authorised” The install lock does not match. Contact your administrator; do not copy the app from another PC.
The invoice is not in the list Check the date range. Lists default to the current month.
The report is empty Check the As of or From/To dates, and confirm the voucher is Posted, not Cancelled.
