Your books, one step at a time

Crown Books · 1.0

Crown Books keeps the accounts of one business on this computer. It answers three questions: who owes us, who do we owe, and do the books balance. This guide shows the actual screens and the exact buttons to press.

01

Who owes us?

Sales invoices, receipts, receivables and ageing.

02

Who do we owe?

Purchase bills, payments, payables and due dates.

03

Do the books balance?

Trial balance, profit and loss, and the balance sheet.

Before you start

  • Business name, address, state and GSTIN.
  • Owner name, email and a password of at least 8 characters.
  • The date your books should begin.
  • Customer and supplier list, with phone and GSTIN where known.
  • Item list with HSN or SAC, unit, GST rate, sale and purchase price.
  • Opening stock, opening party balances and cash or bank balances.
  • Copies of unpaid customer invoices and supplier bills.

How to read a step

Button and field names appear in bold exactly as they appear on screen. Numbers and voucher numbers appear in monospace.

Tip Do one chapter at a time. The first five steps in chapter 1 are enough to post your first invoice. Everything else can wait until you need it.
Important Posted vouchers are never edited. If a bill is wrong, cancel it with a reason and enter a fresh one. The cancelled number is kept forever.

Chapter 01Start here
01

Start here

Open the app, create the company, sign in, and learn the one pattern every voucher follows. By the end of this chapter you will know where everything lives.

1.1 Open the app

Crown Books is installed on this computer. Open it from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut. The app checks that this computer is the one the books were installed for. If you see “This computer is not authorised”, stop and contact your administrator. The application is not designed to be copied to another machine.

There is no internet connection, no cloud account and no sign-in to a website. Everything is stored in a local database file on this computer.

1.2 First-run setup

The first time the app opens, it asks for the company and the owner account. This happens once. You can change the company details later in Admin → Company.

Setup asks forWhy it matters
Business namePrinted on invoices; shown in the sidebar.
Owner name, phone, emailThe first user. The email is the sign-in name.
PasswordAt least 8 characters; stored hashed on this machine.
State and GSTINDecide CGST + SGST or IGST and print the registration on documents.
Books beginThe first day this ledger records transactions; not the financial year.
  1. Business name. Enter the name you want on invoices, for example Crown Traders.
  2. Owner name and Phone (optional). The owner is the first user and can do everything.
  3. Owner email. This becomes your sign-in name. Use an address you check.
  4. Password. At least 8 characters. It is stored hashed on this machine. There is no “forgot password” email in this edition; keep it somewhere safe.
  5. State and GSTIN (optional). The state decides whether a sale is taxed as CGST + SGST or IGST. Enter the GSTIN exactly as registered, without spaces.
  6. Books begin. The first day Crown Books records transactions. See 2.2 before you choose a mid-year date.
  7. Click Create company and sign in.
Books begin is not the financial year The financial year is the reporting period, usually 1 April to 31 March. Books begin is the day this app starts keeping the ledger. If you are starting fresh on 1 April, both dates can be 1 April. If you are migrating in October, books begin is the day after your last entry in the old system, while the financial year is still the current April-to-March year.

1.3 Sign in again

After setup, every launch shows the sign-in screen. Enter the owner email and password and click Sign in. Staff accounts, when they are added in a later edition, will use the same screen.

To leave the app, open the account menu at the top right and choose Sign out. Closing the window is also safe. Nothing is lost when you sign out because every posted voucher is already in the database.

1.4 The screen

Every page has the same frame: a dark navigation rail on the left, a thin top bar, and the page itself. The rail is grouped by the work you are doing, not by database tables.

Rail groupWhat it is for
DashboardThe four numbers: receivables, payables, cash and bank, vouchers posted.

The top bar shows the page name, the current financial year, and your account. The theme button switches between the paper and dark palettes. The search box in the top bar is reserved for a later edition; use the search field on each page instead.

1.5 The anatomy of a voucher

A voucher is any document that changes the books: an invoice, a bill, a receipt, a payment, a journal or a contra. Every entry screen uses the same five-part pattern. Once you know one, you know all of them.

01HeaderParty, date, due date, reference and place of supply.
→
02LinesItem, description, quantity, rate, discount and GST.
→
03TotalsTaxable value, tax, round off and the final total.
→
04PostThe server checks, numbers and writes the voucher.
The one rule that prevents most mistakes Nothing is saved until you press Post. Closing the screen or pressing Cancel discards the entry. The voucher number is assigned only when the post succeeds, and a posted voucher is never edited.

1.6 Your first five steps

If you only want to see a complete cycle, do these five things in order. The rest of the guide explains each one in detail.

  1. Create the company in first-run setup. Use 1 April as books begin if you are starting a fresh year.
  2. Add a customer and a supplier under Parties. One record each is enough to begin.
  3. Add one item under Items. Enter its HSN, GST rate and sale price.
  4. Post a purchase bill under Purchases. This brings stock in and creates the supplier payable.
  5. Post a sales invoice under Sales. Then open the invoice and click Print to see the document.
Next: company settings and opening balances. If this is a brand-new business, you can skip most of chapter 2 and come back when you need to add an account or correct an opening position.
Chapter 02Company and opening balances
02

Company and opening balances

The company profile is printed on every document. The chart of accounts is the list of ledgers behind the reports. Opening balances are the one-time bridge from your old books.

In this chapter
  • Company identity and financial year
  • What “books begin” means
  • Chart of accounts and reserved ledgers
  • Opening balances, posted once
  • Reserved masters

2.1 Company settings

Open Admin → Company. The page has four sections: identity, financial year, stock and rounding, and invoice terms.

FieldWhat to enter
Registered nameThe name printed on invoices, usually the legal or trading name.
GSTIN, PAN, state codeRegistration details. The state code must be two digits, for example 27 for Maharashtra.
Address, city, pincodeThe address printed above the invoice lines.
Financial year startsThe month your reporting year starts. April is the default for India.
Books begin dateThe first day this app records transactions. See 2.2.
Costing methodKeep Weighted average. This edition values stock at weighted average cost.
Round offHow the invoice total is rounded. Invoice total is the usual choice.
Default termsSaved with the company. It is not printed on the current invoice layout in this edition.
Leave this unticked Allow selling below stock on hand is recorded as a setting but the current build does not enforce a stock guard. Keep it unticked and check the stock summary before a large sale.

2.2 What “books begin” means

Books begin is the first day Crown Books is responsible for the ledger. Before that date, the history lives in your old system, your accountant’s file, or nowhere. At that date you bring in one opening position: what you own, what you owe, and what customers owe you.

It is not the financial year. A business can have a financial year from 1 April to 31 March and still begin its Crown Books ledger on 1 October after migrating from Tally. The financial year of an October invoice is still the current April-to-March year.

SituationWhat to enter
Starting a new businessThe day you want the books to start, often 1 April or the day of the first sale.
Fresh financial year1 April of that year. Opening balances are the previous 31 March closing balances.
Migrating mid-yearThe day after the last entry in the old system. Opening balances are as at the close of the day before.
Only using Crown Books for salesStill use the real start date. Do not enter a later date just to avoid old data.
Opening balances are the day before If the old system is closed on 30 September and books begin is 1 October, the opening balances are the position at the close of 30 September. That is the same instant as the start of 1 October. Post the opening voucher with the opening date, then start entering October transactions.

2.3 Chart of accounts

Every voucher writes to ledgers. The chart of accounts is the list of those ledgers, grouped into assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses. Crown Books seeds a working set so you do not have to create one from scratch.

The reserved ledgers are not decoration. Sales, purchases, GST, inventory, cost of goods sold, round off and the opening difference all have a fixed job. You can add your own ledgers for rent, salaries, travel or any other expense without touching the reserved ones.

  1. Click New account.
  2. Enter a Name, for example Office Expenses.
  3. Choose a Group. The group decides the nature; for example Indirect Expenses is an expense group.
  4. Check the Type. It follows the group but can be adjusted.
  5. Leave the opening balance at zero unless you are setting up the opening position.
  6. Click Add account.

To set an opening balance on an existing account, use the Opening balance (INR) and Side fields when the account is created. Dr means a debit balance, Cr a credit balance. If you are not sure which side, ask your accountant before posting.

2.4 Opening balances

Opening balances come from three places: the party record, the account record and the item record. They are collected into one opening voucher so that the trial balance stays balanced.

  1. Add the customer and supplier opening balances in the party records. Positive means the party owes you (Dr); negative means you owe the party (Cr).
  2. Add the opening balance and side on any bank, cash, loan or capital account.
  3. Add opening quantity and opening rate on each goods item. Services do not hold stock.
  4. Open Admin → Company, scroll to Opening balances, and choose the Opening date.
  5. Click Post opening balances. One OPN/0001 voucher is created.
  6. Open the trial balance and check that debits equal credits. If there is a difference, it sits in Opening Balance Difference, which should be investigated, not ignored.
Do not count the same stock twice If opening stock already includes goods, do not enter a purchase bill for those same goods. If you want the purchase history, leave opening stock at zero and enter the historical purchase bills instead. The same rule applies to opening party balances and old invoices.
After the opening voucher The opening fields are locked. Corrections go through a journal, not by editing the opening voucher. This is deliberate: the opening position must stay stable once you have reconciled it.

2.5 Reserved masters

Masters → Tax Rates and Masters → Units appear in the rail but are reserved screens in this edition. GST rates are chosen on each item and can be overridden on an invoice line. Units are seeded (Nos, Pcs, Kg, Ltr, Mtr and others) and chosen on the item. Admin → Users and Roles is also reserved.

Next: parties. If you already entered the customer and supplier list during opening balances, use chapter 3 to check the details and understand the balance column.

2.6 Opening balance checklist

  • The old system is closed and reconciled on the day before books begin.
  • Every customer and supplier opening balance is entered, with the correct Dr or Cr side.
  • Cash, bank, loans and capital are entered on the account records.
  • Opening stock quantity and rate are entered for goods, or left zero if you are bringing purchase history.
  • The opening date matches the day before the first new transaction.
  • The opening voucher is posted once.
  • The trial balance says “Debits equal credits” and Opening Balance Difference is zero or explained.
Migration example The old system has entries up to 30 September. The owner wants Crown Books to start on 1 October. Set Books begin to 1 October, enter balances as at 30 September, and post the opening voucher dated 1 October. The first new sale is dated 1 October or later. The financial year is still the current April-to-March year.
Chapter 03Customers and suppliers
03

Customers and suppliers

One record per business. The same business can be a customer and a supplier. The balance column tells you whether they owe you or you owe them.

In this chapter
  • One record per business
  • Create a party
  • Edit and deactivate
  • Read the party list
  • Credit days and credit limit

3.1 One record per business

A party is a customer, a supplier, or both. Use one record for a business even if you buy from and sell to them. The role is chosen when the party is created and is not changed later, because the role decides which ledger accounts the party uses.

The balance is shown as Dr or Cr. For a customer, Dr means they owe you. For a supplier, Cr means you owe them. A negative customer balance or a positive supplier balance is possible after an advance or an overpayment; it is not automatically an error.

3.2 Create a party

Open Parties → All Parties and click New party.

  1. Name. The trading name you will recognise in lists, for example Gupta Traders.
  2. Phone and Email. Optional, but useful when chasing a bill.
  3. GSTIN. Enter the 15-character registration number. Leave blank for an unregistered party.
  4. State code. Two digits. This is used to decide CGST + SGST or IGST on a sale.
  5. Billing address. Printed on documents when available.
  6. Credit days. The default due date on a new invoice. 30 means the invoice is due 30 days after the invoice date.
  7. Credit limit (INR). A reference limit. It does not block a sale in this edition.
  8. Opening balance (INR). Positive if the party owes you, negative if you owe the party. Leave zero for a new party.
  9. Role. Tick Customer, Supplier, or both.
  10. Click Add party.

3.3 Edit and deactivate

Use Edit on a row to correct a phone number, address, credit days or credit limit. The role and the opening balance are fixed after the party is created. If a party is no longer used, click Deactivate. The history stays in the books; the party stops appearing in new-entry lists.

Tip Deactivate a party instead of renaming it to “OLD”. Reports and old vouchers keep the original name and history, and the list stays readable.

3.4 Read the party list

The search box matches name, phone and GSTIN. The role buttons filter to All, Customers or Suppliers. Show inactive reveals deactivated records. The Credit days column is the default payment term, not the age of the current balance.

3.5 Credit days and credit limit

Credit days are copied to the due date when you choose the party on an invoice. You can change the due date for an individual invoice. Ageing reports use the due date by default, so a wrong credit term makes a bill look overdue earlier or later than it really is.

Credit limit is a reference figure for your team. In this edition it does not stop an invoice from being posted. If a customer is near the limit, the person entering the sale must check.

3.6 Party checklist

  • The legal or trading name is the one you will recognise later.
  • GSTIN is 15 characters and the state code is two digits.
  • Customer, supplier or both is chosen correctly; the role cannot change later.
  • Credit days match the agreed terms.
  • Opening balance is zero for a new party, or the correct Dr / Cr amount for a migration.
  • Phone and email are present if the team chases bills.
  • The party appears in the right filter: Customers, Suppliers or All Parties.

Which role should I choose?

RoleUse it when
CustomerYou sell to them. They appear in Sales and Receivables.
SupplierYou buy from them. They appear in Purchases and Payables.
BothYou buy and sell to the same business. One record keeps the net balance in one place.

Chapter 04Items and stock
04

Items and stock

An item is anything you buy or sell: a physical product with stock, or a service with no stock. The item carries the HSN or SAC, the unit, the GST rate and the default prices.

In this chapter
  • Goods and services
  • Create an item
  • Opening stock
  • The item list
  • The stock summary
  • How stock moves

4.1 Goods and services

A goods item carries stock. Buying it increases quantity, selling it decreases quantity, and the stock summary shows what is left. A service item has no stock; it is used for maintenance, consulting or any other work billed by quantity, hours or a flat rate.

4.2 Create an item

Open Items → Items and click New item.

  1. Name. The name that appears on the invoice, for example Wireless Keyboard.
  2. SKU. Your internal code. Optional but useful for searching.
  3. HSN / SAC. The classification code for GST. Goods use HSN, services use SAC.
  4. Unit. Nos, Pcs, Kg, Ltr, Mtr and others are seeded.
  5. GST rate. 0, 5, 12, 18 or 28 percent. The rate is copied to a new invoice line but can be changed there.
  6. Service item (no stock). Tick for services.
  7. Sale price and Purchase price (INR). These are defaults, not locked prices.
  8. Opening qty and Opening rate (INR). Only for goods you already hold. These are fixed after the item is created.
  9. Reorder level. A reference level for purchasing. It does not create an alert in this edition.
  10. Click Add item.

4.3 Opening stock

Opening quantity and opening rate are included in the one-time opening voucher. They are not a purchase bill. If you enter opening stock and then also enter a purchase bill for the same goods, stock and cost will be counted twice.

Choose one method Opening stock only: enter what you hold now and start buying from today. Full purchase history: leave opening stock at zero and enter the old purchase bills. Do not mix the two for the same goods.

4.4 The item list

The list shows HSN or SAC, unit, GST rate, stock on hand, sale price and purchase price. Use the search box for name, SKU or HSN. Edit changes the descriptive fields and default prices; opening quantity and rate stay fixed. Deactivate hides an item from new entries without deleting history.

4.5 Stock summary

Open Items → Stock Summary. This is a computed report, not a data-entry screen. It reads every posted purchase, sale and return.

Quantity is what is left. Value is quantity multiplied by the weighted average cost of the item. The total at the bottom is the inventory value that appears on the balance sheet. Services are not shown because they hold no stock.

4.6 How stock moves

ActionEffect on stockEffect on the books
Post a purchase billQuantity increases.Creditor increases, inventory increases, input GST is recorded.
Post a sales invoiceQuantity decreases.Customer owes you, sales and output GST increase, cost of goods sold is recorded.
Cancel a purchase or saleThe original movement is reversed.The ledger effect is reversed; the voucher stays with status Cancelled.
Enter opening stockQuantity starts at the opening figure.Inventory and the opening difference are recorded once.
Why the stock value can change without a new purchase The costing method is weighted average. When a new purchase arrives at a different rate, the average cost of the remaining stock changes, so the value of the same quantity can move.

5.1 The buying flow

01Purchase billStock comes in. The supplier becomes a creditor.
→
02PaymentMoney goes out. Allocate it to the bill it settles.
→
03PayablesWhat is still owed, split into not due and overdue.

5.2 New purchase bill

Open Books → Purchases and click New bill. The screen is a bill, not a payment. Enter what the supplier billed you.

  1. Party. Choose the supplier. The list shows the current balance and credit days.
  2. Date. The bill date. This decides the financial year and the register period.
  3. Due date. Filled from the supplier’s credit days. Change it if the bill says otherwise.
  4. Reference. The supplier’s bill number, for example TC/26-27/604.
  5. Place of supply. The supplier’s state code. Same state gives CGST + SGST; a different state gives IGST.
  6. Add line for each item. Choose the item or type a free-text description.
  7. Enter Qty, Rate, Disc % and GST %. HSN and unit fill from the item but can be changed.
  8. Check Taxable value, the tax split, Round off and Total.
  9. Add a Narration that will make sense in six months.
  10. Click Post bill. The number, for example PUR/0001, is assigned at that moment.
Line fieldWhat it means
ItemA saved item, or Free text for a one-off purchase. A saved goods item moves stock.
DescriptionThe words printed on the bill. Defaults to the item name.
HSNThe GST classification. Defaults from the item.
Qty and UnitHow much was received. Up to four decimal places.
RateThe purchase rate before GST.
Disc %Line discount. Leave 0 if there is none.
GST %The tax rate for this line. It can differ from the item default.

5.3 What posting does

  • The supplier’s payable increases by the bill total.
  • Inventory increases by the taxable value of goods lines; services post to the expense or purchase ledger without stock.
  • Input CGST, SGST or IGST is recorded according to the place of supply.
  • The voucher gets a permanent number and appears in the purchase register and payables.
The bill is not the payment Posting a purchase bill does not move money. It records the obligation. Use a payment voucher when the money actually leaves.

5.4 Print a purchase bill

Open Purchases, click the bill number, and use Print. The print dialog also lets you choose Save as PDF if you need a file for email or records. The printed view carries the company name, supplier, bill number, lines, tax and total.

5.5 Pay a supplier

Open Books → Payments and click New payment. A payment can settle one bill, several bills, or stay on account as an advance.

  1. Party. Choose the supplier. The screen shows the current balance.
  2. Date. The date money left, not the bill date.
  3. Amount. The total paid, for example 25,000.
  4. Method and Reference. Choose NEFT, UPI, cheque or cash and enter the bank reference.
  5. The Open bills table lists unpaid purchase bills with their due dates and outstanding amounts.
  6. Enter the amount against the bill being settled in Allocate. Leave the rest unallocated if it is an advance.
  7. Check Paid, Allocated and On account at the bottom.
  8. Click Post payment.
SituationWhat to do
Pay one bill in fullAllocate the full outstanding amount to that bill.
Part paymentAllocate less than the outstanding. The rest stays open.
Pay several billsEnter an allocation against each bill in the same payment.
AdvanceLeave the allocation blank. The money sits on account until a bill arrives.
Pay more than the billAllocate the bill amount and leave the excess on account.
Cash and bank in this edition Receipts and payments post to the Cash ledger. The Method list is a label on the entry screen. To move money between cash and bank, use Journals → New contra after the receipt or payment. Bank reconciliation is not part of this edition.

5.6 Payables

Open Reports → Payables. The report shows who you owe, split into amounts not yet due and amounts past the due date.

As of is the date you are looking at. Basis decides whether a bill is measured by its bill date or its due date. Use Due date for collection and payment planning; use Bill date when you want the age of the document itself.

5.7 Purchase register

Open Reports → Purchase Register from the report links or navigate directly. It lists every purchase bill in the period with taxable value, GST, round off and total.

5.8 Buying checklist

  • Supplier chosen, not the customer with a similar name.
  • Bill date and due date match the supplier’s bill.
  • Supplier’s bill number entered in Reference.
  • Place of supply is the supplier’s state.
  • Each line has the right quantity and rate.
  • GST rate matches the item, not the default from another item.
  • Total matches the paper bill to the paisa.
Next: selling. The sales screen is the mirror image, with one important difference: the place of supply is the customer’s state, and it decides CGST + SGST or IGST.

6.1 The selling flow

01Sales invoiceCustomer owes you. Stock goes out. GST is recorded.
→
02ReceiptMoney comes in. Allocate it to the invoice it settles.
→
03ReceivablesWhat is still owed, with ageing and due dates.

6.2 New sales invoice

Open Books → Sales and click New invoice. Every sale is against a customer; a cash sale is recorded as an invoice followed by a receipt.

Before you invoice

  • The customer record exists and the GSTIN is correct.
  • You know the customer’s state for the place of supply.
  • The item has the right GST rate and sale price.
  • The goods are in stock, or you have decided to sell anyway.
  • The order or purchase order number is in front of you.
  1. Party. Choose the customer. The screen shows their balance, credit days and GSTIN.
  2. Date. The invoice date. This decides the financial year and the sales register.
  3. Due date. Filled from the customer’s credit days. Change it if the order says otherwise.
  4. Reference. The customer’s purchase order number, if any.
  5. Place of supply. The customer’s state code. Same state as your company gives CGST + SGST; a different state gives IGST.
  6. Add line for each item. Choose the saved item or type a free-text description.
  7. Enter Qty, Rate, Disc % and GST %.
  8. Check the totals and add a Narration.
  9. Click Post invoice. The number, for example INV/0001, is assigned on posting.
Place of supply is the most common GST mistake The place of supply defaults to your company’s state. For an out-of-state customer, change it to the customer’s state before posting. If you leave it on your own state, the invoice will charge CGST + SGST instead of IGST and the GST return will be wrong.

6.3 GST and place of supply

GST is charged per line. The place of supply decides how the same 18 percent is split.

Place of supplyWhat is chargedExample
Same state as the companyCGST + SGST, half the rate each.Company in 27 (Maharashtra), customer in 27. 18% becomes 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
Different stateIGST at the full rate.Company in 27, customer in 29 (Karnataka). 18% becomes 18% IGST.

The tax is calculated on the taxable value after the line discount. The total is rounded according to the company setting, usually at the invoice total. The summary on the right shows taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST, round off and total before you post.

6.4 Print or save the invoice

Open Sales, click the invoice number, and use Print. In the system print dialog, choose a printer or Save as PDF. The printed page carries the company name and GSTIN, the customer, the invoice number and date, the line items with HSN, the tax split and the total.

6.5 Receipt from a customer

Open Books → Receipts and click New receipt. A receipt can settle one invoice, several invoices, or stay on account as an advance.

  1. Party. Choose the customer. The screen shows the current balance.
  2. Date. The date money was received.
  3. Amount. The total received.
  4. Method and Reference. UPI, NEFT, cheque or cash, and the reference number.
  5. The Open invoices table lists unpaid invoices with due dates and outstanding amounts.
  6. Enter the amount against the invoice being settled in Allocate.
  7. Check Received, Allocated and On account.
  8. Click Post receipt.
Advances and part payments A receipt larger than the open invoice leaves the excess on account. A receipt with no allocation is an advance. When the next invoice is posted, use a new receipt or adjust the advance through a journal if your accountant prefers.

6.6 Receivables and ageing

Open Reports → Receivables for the customer totals, and Reports → Ageing for the age buckets.

Receivables shows two numbers per customer: not yet due, and overdue. Ageing splits the overdue amount into buckets. Use the Basis selector to age by due date (the default) or by bill date. A bill that is not yet due sits in Not due even if the invoice is old.

Who to chase first Start with the largest amount in 61–90 and 91–180, not the largest total. Then check 31–60 for customers who are about to become a bigger problem. A 180+ balance needs a decision: collect, write off, or ask the accountant.

6.7 Sales register

The sales register lists every invoice in a period with taxable value, GST, round off and total. It is the first check before a GST return or a management review.

  • Every invoice in the period is present, and none is cancelled by mistake.
  • The total GST agrees with the output tax in the trial balance.
  • The invoice with the largest value has the right place of supply.

6.8 Selling checklist

  • Customer chosen, not a supplier with a similar name.
  • Invoice date and due date are correct.
  • Place of supply is the customer’s state, not the company’s default.
  • Each line has the right quantity, rate and GST rate.
  • Discount, if any, is on the correct line.
  • Total matches the order or the quotation.
  • Receipt allocated to the invoice, not left on account by mistake.
  • Invoice printed or saved as PDF and sent to the customer.

Chapter 07Adjustments
07

Adjustments and corrections

The books keep a history. A mistake is corrected by cancelling the voucher and entering a new one, or by posting a journal. Nothing is deleted.

In this chapter
  • Posted means posted
  • Cancel a voucher
  • Journal adjustments
  • Contra: cash to bank
  • The journals list
  • When to ask your accountant

7.1 Posted means posted

A posted voucher is part of the audit trail. You cannot edit its lines, party, date or amount. If it is wrong, cancel it with a reason and enter a fresh voucher. The cancelled voucher stays visible with status CANCELLED, its number is not reused, and its ledger and stock effect is reversed.

7.2 Cancel a voucher

  1. Open the voucher from Sales, Purchases, Receipts, Payments, Day Book or a report link.
  2. Click Cancel voucher at the top right.
  3. Enter a Reason of at least three characters. “Wrong party” is enough; “test” is not useful.
  4. Click Cancel voucher in the dialog. The status becomes CANCELLED.
  5. Enter the corrected voucher from the beginning.
Do not cancel just to hide a bill Cancellation reverses the ledger and stock effect, but the voucher remains in the day book with its reason. It is for corrections, not for removing a real transaction from history.

7.3 Journal

A journal is a manual double entry. Use it for adjustments that are not a sale, purchase, receipt or payment: owner capital, depreciation, an expense paid from cash, or an accountant’s correction.

Every journal needs at least two lines, and the total debits must equal the total credits. The screen shows Balanced in green only when they match. The Post button stays disabled when the journal is not balanced.

Before you journal Write down the two account names and the amount before you open the screen. If you cannot name both accounts, stop and ask the accountant. A journal entered to “see if it works” is harder to clean up than a phone call.
ExampleDebitCredit
Owner introduces capitalCashOwner Capital
Office expense paid in cashOffice ExpensesCash
Depreciation for the monthDepreciationAccumulated Depreciation
Correct an over-posted expenseThe account that was wrongly debitedThe expense account

If you are unsure which accounts to use, do not guess. A wrong journal is harder to find than a missing one. Ask the accountant to give you the two account names and the amount.

7.4 Contra

A contra moves money between two cash or bank accounts. The common case is depositing cash into the bank. It is not a receipt or a payment, so it does not affect a customer or supplier.

  1. Open Books → Journals and click New contra.
  2. Choose the From account, usually Cash.
  3. Choose the To account, usually Bank.
  4. Enter the Date and Amount.
  5. Add a Narration, for example “Cash deposited into the bank”.
  6. Click Post.

7.5 The journals list

The Journals page lists both manual journals and contra transfers, newest first. The Type column tells you which one it is; the Number column is the voucher you can quote to your accountant.

7.6 When to ask your accountant

  • Opening balances do not tie to the old trial balance.
  • A GST return is due and the output or input tax looks wrong.
  • You need depreciation, prepaid expenses, provisions or any year-end adjustment.
  • A party balance is negative or a supplier balance is positive and you cannot explain it.
  • The trial balance does not say “Debits equal credits.”

Which correction do I need?

The mistakeWhat to do
Wrong party, date, item or amount on a posted billCancel it with a reason and enter the correct voucher.
Wrong GST rate or place of supply on a posted invoiceCancel and re-enter. Do not patch it with a journal; the GST return needs the corrected invoice.
Expense posted to the wrong ledgerPost a journal: debit the correct expense, credit the wrong one.
Owner put money into the businessJournal: debit Cash or Bank, credit Owner Capital.
Cash deposited into the bankContra: from Cash, to Bank. Not a receipt or payment.

Chapter 08Reports
08

Reports

Reports are reads of posted vouchers. They do not store totals, so a cancelled voucher disappears from the report and a new one appears immediately.

In this chapter
  • How reports work
  • Day book
  • Trial balance
  • Profit and loss
  • Balance sheet
  • Which report answers which question

8.1 How reports work

Every report reads the posted vouchers for the current company. A cancelled voucher is excluded from the ledger effect. The date controls mean different things:

ControlMeaning
From / ToA period, for example 1 April to 22 October. Used by profit and loss, balance sheet and registers.
As ofA single date. Used by receivables, payables and ageing: what was open on that day.
BasisAge by due date (default) or by bill date. Due date is better for collection; bill date is better for document age.
Three checks before you trust a report
  1. Is the voucher posted, not cancelled?
  2. Is the date range or as-of date the one you meant?
  3. Does the trial balance still say “Debits equal credits”?

8.2 Day book

The day book lists every voucher in date order, whatever its type. It is the first place to look when you cannot find a document.

The date range defaults to the current month. Change From and To for an older period, use Search for a number or party, and filter by Status to see only posted or cancelled documents.

  • The newest voucher is at the top of the list.
  • The number is the permanent one; quote it when asking for help.
  • A cancelled voucher remains visible with status CANCELLED.

8.3 Trial balance

The trial balance lists every account with a debit or credit balance. If the total of the debit column equals the total of the credit column, the ledger is internally consistent. It does not prove that the business made a profit; it proves that every voucher had two sides.

Read it by group. Assets and expenses normally sit on the debit side; liabilities, equity and income on the credit side. A party can appear on either side because the balance is net.

8.4 Profit and loss

Profit and loss answers one question: did the business make money in this period? It shows income, expenses and the net profit. The period is controlled by From and To.

Sales is shown net of returns. Cost of goods sold is the weighted average cost of the stock that left. Expenses include round off and any journals you posted to expense accounts. Net profit is the green figure at the bottom; a loss is shown in red.

8.5 Balance sheet

The balance sheet is a photograph at a date: what the business owns, what it owes, and the owner’s stake. It must satisfy:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets

Cash, bank, stock, money customers owe you, and things the business owns.

Liabilities and equity

Suppliers, loans, GST payable, owner capital and the profit kept in the business.

Inventory is valued at weighted average cost. Customer and supplier balances are net. The current period profit is added to equity automatically, so the sheet balances without a manual closing entry.

8.6 Report map

Chapter 09Backup and safety
09

Backup and safety

The books live in one SQLite file on this computer. A backup is a copy of that file. Restore is deliberately manual so that a live database is never swapped underneath the app.

In this chapter
  • Create a backup
  • Restore a backup
  • When to back up
  • Safety rules
  • This computer only

9.1 Create a backup

Open Admin → Backup and click Create backup. The app writes a timestamped copy, for example crown-books-2026-10-22T09-15-00-000Z.db, into the backup folder next to the database.

The file name, creation time and size are listed. A backup is a complete copy; it is not a delta. Keep at least one copy on a different drive or a USB stick, not only on the same laptop.

9.2 Restore a backup

Close the app first Restoring while Crown Books is open can corrupt the database. Close the app completely, including any window in the taskbar, before replacing the file.
  1. Create a fresh backup of the current books, even if they are wrong. It is your safety net.
  2. Close Crown Books.
  3. Open the app data folder. On Windows this is usually %APPDATA%\crown-books.
  4. Rename the current crown-books.db to crown-books.before-restore.db.
  5. Copy the chosen backup from the backups folder into the app data folder.
  6. Rename the copy to crown-books.db.
  7. Open Crown Books and sign in. Check the dashboard, trial balance and a recent invoice.

The backup folder is usually %APPDATA%\crown-books\backups. If your installation uses a different folder, ask your administrator before moving files.

9.3 When to back up

WhenWhy
Before the first real billSo you can return to an empty, known state.
Every day after postingThe day’s work is the most expensive thing to lose.
Before a GST returnFreeze the numbers you are about to file.
Before a restore or upgradeKeep a copy of the current state even if it is imperfect.
Every Friday, off the machineProtect against disk failure, theft or ransomware.
At month endA clean copy for the accountant.

9.4 Safety rules

  • Do not edit the database with another program while Crown Books is open.
  • Do not copy the app folder to another computer. The install lock will refuse to run, and the data may not be the same.
  • Do not restore a backup over a live database. Close the app first.
  • Do not keep every backup on the same laptop. Use a second drive or a USB stick.

9.5 This computer only

Crown Books is offline by design. There is no cloud sync, no remote login and no automatic off-site copy. That is a feature for privacy and a risk for disaster recovery. The backup routine is the disaster recovery plan.

Test a restore before you need it

  • Create a backup and note the file name.
  • Close the app and follow 9.2 on a quiet day.
  • Open the dashboard and check the receivables figure.
  • Open the most recent invoice and confirm it prints.
  • Post one test receipt, then cancel it and note the reason.
  • Keep the pre-restore copy until you are satisfied.
The 3-2-1 rule, in plain language Keep 3 copies, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 copy off the computer. For a small shop, that can be the live database, a daily backup on the laptop, and a weekly copy on a USB drive kept elsewhere.

10.5 Daily, weekly and monthly routine

Every day

  • Post today’s sales invoices.
  • Post supplier bills as they arrive.
  • Enter receipts and payments, with allocations.
  • Create a backup at the end of the day.

Every week

  • Open Receivables and chase the oldest overdue bills.
  • Open Payables and plan the week’s payments.
  • Check Stock Summary for items below reorder level.
  • Copy the latest backup to a USB drive or another machine.

Every month

  • Check the Trial Balance says “Debits equal credits.”
  • Read Profit and Loss and compare it with the previous month.
  • Read the Balance Sheet and explain any large movement.
  • Reconcile the bank statement outside the app; use contra for transfers.
  • Create a month-end backup for the accountant.

Every year

  • Confirm the financial year start month in Company settings.
  • Take a full backup before the new year’s first entry.
  • Ask the accountant about closing journals and depreciation.
