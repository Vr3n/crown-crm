10.2Voucher series and status

Series Document What it does
INV/ Sales invoice Customer owes you; stock goes out; output GST.
PUR/ Purchase bill You owe the supplier; stock comes in; input GST.
RCT/ Receipt Money received from a customer.
PAY/ Payment Money paid to a supplier.
JV/ Journal Manual adjustment; debits must equal credits.
CTR/ Contra Cash to bank or bank to cash.
OPN/ Opening The one-time opening balances voucher.
CN/ · DN/ Credit / debit note Reserved routes; not exposed as a screen.

Status is either Posted or Cancelled. Posted documents count in reports. Cancelled documents stay visible but their ledger and stock effect is reversed. A number is never reused.

10.3Messages and fixes

Message or symptom What it means and what to do
“Debits equal credits.” The trial balance is consistent. No action.
“The books do not balance.” Stop. Take a backup and ask your accountant; do not post more entries to hide it.
“Opening balances have already been posted” Only one opening voucher is allowed. Use a journal for corrections.
“Choose customer, supplier, or both” The party role is missing. Edit the party while it is being created; roles are fixed after saving.
“GSTIN must be 15 characters” Count the characters. Remove spaces and dashes.
“State code: two digits” Use 27, not “Maharashtra” or “MH”.
“Password must be at least 8 characters” Use a longer password. There is no email reset in this edition.
“This computer is not authorised” The install lock does not match. Contact your administrator; do not copy the app from another PC.
The invoice is not in the list Check the date range. Lists default to the current month.
The report is empty Check the As of or From/To dates, and confirm the voucher is Posted, not Cancelled.

10.4Glossary

Word Plain meaning
Receivable Money a customer owes you.
Payable Money you owe a supplier.
Debtor The customer side of receivables.
Creditor The supplier side of payables.
Debit (Dr) One side of every entry. A customer balance in Dr means they owe you.
Credit (Cr) The other side. A supplier balance in Cr means you owe them.
Ledger A named account, for example Cash or Sales.
Voucher Any posted document: invoice, bill, receipt, payment, journal or contra.
Narration The note on the voucher that explains it.
Allocation Linking a receipt or payment to the bill it settles.
Word Plain meaning
On account Money received or paid that is not yet linked to a bill.
Ageing How old unpaid bills are, in 30-day buckets.
HSN / SAC The GST classification code for goods / services.
CGST / SGST The two halves of GST on a sale inside one state.
IGST GST on a sale across state lines.
COGS Cost of goods sold: what the items you sold cost you.
Opening balance The position brought forward on the first day of the books.
Contra A transfer between cash and bank.
Journal A manual adjustment with matched debits and credits.
Trial balance Every account balance, used to prove debits equal credits.

10.5Daily, weekly and monthly routine

When Do this
Every day Post today’s sales invoices and supplier bills. Enter receipts and payments, with allocations. Create a backup at the end of the day.
Every week Chase the oldest overdue bills. Plan the week’s payments. Check stock below reorder level. Copy the latest backup off the machine.
Every month Check that the Trial Balance says “Debits equal credits.” Compare Profit and Loss with last month. Explain any large Balance Sheet movement. Create a month-end backup for the accountant.
Every year Confirm the financial year start month. Take a full backup before the first entry. Ask the accountant about closing journals and depreciation.

10.7Where to get help

Inside the business

  • The owner or administrator for access, installation and backups.
  • The accountant for journals, GST, depreciation and year-end entries.
  • The backup custodian for the weekly copy kept off the machine.

Before you call

  • The voucher number and date, if the problem is a document.
  • What you expected and what happened.
  • A screenshot of the message, if there is one.
  • Whether you have a backup from today.
