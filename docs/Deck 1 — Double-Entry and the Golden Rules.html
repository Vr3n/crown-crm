Core concept 1 of 3 · Double-entry bookkeeping

Every deal has two sides.

The three golden rules of accounting. Built from first principles, step by step.

1 · The base idea

Someone paid for everything the business owns

Assets=Liabilities+Equity

Assets are what the business owns. Liabilities are what it owes. Equity is what is left for the owner.

Assets
Cash ₹100,000Cash ₹20,000
Paid by
Owner equity ₹100,000Loan ₹20,000

The owner puts in ₹100,000. Assets go up. Equity goes up by the same amount.

The bank lends ₹20,000. Assets go up. Liabilities go up by the same amount. Both sides stay equal.

2 · The two sides

Each deal changes two accounts. One is debit. One is credit.

Cash A/c

DEBIT (Dr) · left
100,000Owner invests
CREDIT (Cr) · right
30,000Buy furniture

Balance: ₹70,000 Dr

Two words. No moral meaning.

Debit means the left side. Credit means the right side.

Debit does not mean decrease. Credit does not mean increase. The account type decides.

The total of all debits must always equal the total of all credits.

3 · Account types

Three traditional types cover every account

Personal

A person or an organisation.

Amit (customer), Ravi (supplier), Capital (owner), the bank.

Real

A thing the business owns.

Cash, stock, furniture, machines, land. Also goodwill and patents.

Nominal

An income, gain, expense, or loss.

Sales, rent, salary, commission, interest, discount.

Personal and real accounts stay open year after year. Nominal accounts close at year end. They feed the profit statement.

4 · Golden rule 1 · Personal account

Debit the receiver. Credit the giver.

You pay ₹5,000 in cash to your supplier Ravi.

Ravi receives money. So we debit Ravi. His account shows less owed to him.

Cash goes out. So we credit Cash (rule 2).

5 · Golden rule 2 · Real account

Debit what comes in. Credit what goes out.

You buy furniture for ₹30,000 and pay in cash.

Cashreal account₹30,000
→
Furniturereal account
AccountDebitCredit
Furniture A/c Dr30,000
To Cash A/c30,000

Furniture comes in. Debit it. One asset goes up.

Cash goes out. Credit it. Another asset goes down. Total assets did not change.

6 · Golden rule 3 · Nominal account

Debit all expenses and losses. Credit all incomes and gains.

Pay rent ₹5,000 (expense)

Rent A/c Dr5,000
To Cash A/c5,000

Rent is an expense. Debit it. Cash goes out. Credit it.

Earn commission ₹8,000 (income)

Cash A/c Dr8,000
To Commission A/c8,000

Cash comes in. Debit it. Commission is income. Credit it.

Expenses reduce equity, so they sit on the debit side. Incomes raise equity, so they sit on the credit side.

Assets and expenses have a debit normal balance. Liabilities, equity, and income have a credit normal balance. Your software stores this on each account.

10 · In your software

The software must refuse any entry that does not balance

Two lines at least

One voucher posts two or more ledger lines. Each line has an account and one amount on one side.

Balance check

Before commit: sum of debits = sum of credits. If not, the database rejects the post.

Typed accounts

Each account has a type (asset, liability, equity, income, expense). The type gives its normal side.

No edits

A posted line is final. A mistake is fixed by a reversing entry. Staff see simple bills, not Dr and Cr.

Account balance = total debits − total credits. It is always computed. It is never typed by hand.

Summary

Five sentences to remember

1

Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Every deal keeps this true.

2

Every deal has a debit side (left) and a credit side (right). Totals match.

3

Personal: debit the receiver, credit the giver.

4

Real: debit what comes in, credit what goes out.

5

Nominal: debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.
