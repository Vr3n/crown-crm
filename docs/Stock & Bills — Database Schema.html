Almost every row names the company it belongs to

1

Gym tables carry organization_id. Trader tables carry company_id.

2

One owner can run two firms with two GSTINs. Their books must never mix.

3

Voucher numbers restart each financial year in each series. The unique key is company + series + year + number.

This one column gives you multi-company books like Tally. Add it now. It is hard to add later.

Four habits keep the books correct for years

1

History is added. A posted bill is locked. Cancel and reissue. Items are switched off, never deleted.

2

Idempotency. Each post has a unique key. A double click posts once.

3

Guards. The database refuses bad rows: debit and credit together, or an allocation above the amount owed.

4

Rules are rows. GST rates, HSN codes, and voucher series have an effective date. A new rate is a new row.

Not copied from the gym: membership, lead pipeline, facility access. Keep the join-table idea for user roles per company.

Five sentences to take away

1

Every row names its company. One party row serves customer and supplier.

2

A bill line copies name, HSN, rate, and GST. Old bills never change.

3

One voucher posts ledger lines and stock movements together.

4

Payments reach bills through a bridge. Status and stock are computed.

5

Money is whole paise. Posts are idempotent. History is added.
